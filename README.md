# NeoMundi × ASRO — Round 2 Methodology Protocol

## Purpose

This private repository documents Round 2 of the NeoMundi × ASRO methodological collaboration.

The purpose of this round is to examine how NeoMundi runtime observations can be documented, interpreted and bounded in a way that preserves clear distinctions between:

* measurement;
* observation;
* interpretation;
* attestation;
* governance authority;
* downstream action.

## Core Question

Can a runtime measurement record remain useful for governance, audit and reconstruction without overstating what the measurement itself proves?

## Why This Matters

Runtime signals can reveal instability, variability, factual-risk indicators, measurement limitations or changes in observed conditions.

However, a runtime signal is not automatically:

* a truth claim;
* a safety certification;
* an authorization;
* a governance decision;
* an execution instruction.

Round 2 therefore focuses on the methodological discipline required to preserve the meaning, limits and reconstructibility of a measurement record when it may later inform governance, audit or downstream interpretation.

## Scope

Round 2 is a methodological and non-production exercise.

It does not involve:

* customer data;
* proprietary prompts or outputs;
* live production decisions;
* legal certification;
* delegated governance authority;
* automatic execution control;
* public performance claims.

The work is intended to clarify boundaries, improve documentation discipline and identify the conditions under which records can remain interpretable and reconstructible.

## Roles

### NeoMundi

NeoMundi provides structured runtime observation records, including behavioral stability, coherence, risk signals, measurement coverage, continuity indicators and documented limitations.

NeoMundi’s role is to make observed runtime conditions visible and traceable.

NeoMundi does not claim that an observation record alone determines truth, safety, admissibility, authority or execution permission.

### ASRO

ASRO provides methodological review on proof discipline, attestation boundaries, artifact reliability, authority separation and the conditions under which a record can remain interpretable and reconstructible.

ASRO’s contribution focuses on clarifying what a measurement can support, what it cannot support, and how the distinction between observation, interpretation, attestation and authority can remain explicit.

Neither party’s contribution should be interpreted as transferring operational authority to the other.

## Round 2 Objectives

Round 2 aims to:

1. clarify the distinction between measurement, observation, interpretation, attestation and authority;
2. identify the minimum information required for a runtime observation record to remain interpretable;
3. examine how measurement limitations, coverage gaps and contextual boundaries should be documented;
4. review the reliability and reconstructibility of supporting artifacts;
5. strengthen the methodological foundations of future interoperability and governance formats;
6. document unresolved questions, exclusions and next steps.

## Expected Deliverables

Round 2 is expected to produce:

1. a clarified measurement and attestation boundary;
2. a reviewed set of methodological questions and findings;
3. a documented distinction between observed state, interpretation and authority;
4. a traceable review record;
5. a short synthesis of conclusions, limitations and next steps;
6. recommendations relevant to future NeoMundi governance and interoperability artifacts.

## Relation to the Wider Governance Research Program

This Round 2 protocol forms one methodological track within NeoMundi’s broader research on runtime observation, admissibility and execution-time governance.

Related exercises may examine complementary questions, including:

* how runtime signals can inform governed admissibility;
* how observed uncertainty or discontinuity may affect execution boundaries;
* how decisions and their underlying conditions can remain reconstructible;
* how measurement, interpretation, authority and consequence can remain clearly separated.

ASRO’s specific role in this Round 2 is to examine proof discipline, attestation boundaries, artifact reliability and authority separation.

Other contributors may explore complementary dimensions of the same wider research question, without being part of this ASRO protocol or being represented as a single joint framework.

## Working Principles

This collaboration follows several practical principles:

* **Measurement is not authority.**
* **A score is not a decision.**
* **A record must retain its limits, not only its signals.**
* **Interpretation should remain distinguishable from observation.**
* **Attestation should not be implied where it has not been produced.**
* **No contributor should be represented beyond their explicit scope of participation.**
* **Methodological findings should remain traceable to their underlying artifacts and assumptions.**
